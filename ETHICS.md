# Ethics

## Purpose

Body of Evidence exists to make complex investigations transparent and reproducible. This creates real responsibilities: to the people who are subjects of investigations, to the people who use this platform, and to the broader project of public knowledge.

This document states the ethical principles that govern the platform's operation.

---

## Core Commitments

### Accuracy Over Speed

We do not publish findings until they meet the confidence and provenance standards in [METHODOLOGY.md](METHODOLOGY.md). A wrong finding, published early, causes harm. A correct finding, published late, is still correct.

### Transparency Over Advocacy

The platform presents evidence. It does not argue for a conclusion. If the evidence is ambiguous, we record the ambiguity. If the evidence strongly supports a conclusion, we say so — and we say why. We do not shade the evidence to support a preferred outcome.

### Proportionality

Findings that implicate individuals in serious conduct require correspondingly strong evidence. The confidence threshold for serious allegations is higher, not lower, than for routine factual claims.

### Harm Awareness

Investigations can harm individuals — even when the findings are accurate. We do not include personal information about private individuals beyond what is necessary for the investigation. We consider the potential harm of publication against the public interest served by publication.

### Source Protection

Where a source has requested anonymity, we record the source's existence and provenance internally without publishing identifying information. We do not expose confidential sources in the public repository. The fact of anonymisation is itself disclosed.

## Conflicts of Interest

### Disclosure Requirement

Anyone who contributes to an investigation must disclose any conflict of interest: financial relationships, personal relationships, professional relationships, ideological commitments, or prior public positions that could reasonably be seen to affect their objectivity.

Disclosure is made in the PR or issue. It does not automatically disqualify a contribution — disclosed conflicts are manageable. Undisclosed conflicts are not.

### Recusal

A contributor with a material conflict of interest in an investigation should not be the sole reviewer of contributions to that investigation. Maintainers with material conflicts recuse themselves from merge decisions.

### What Counts as a Conflict

- Financial interest in any party to the investigation
- Current or former employment by any party
- Close personal or family relationship with any subject
- Prior public advocacy for a conclusion the investigation is examining
- Receipt of material benefit from any party

When in doubt, disclose.

---

## Subjects of Investigations

### Private vs. Public Figures

Public figures (politicians, executives, public officials, public institutions) are subject to scrutiny of their public conduct. Private individuals are treated with substantially more care. Personal information about private individuals is included only when directly necessary to the investigation and when there is a clear public interest.

### Right of Response

Where an investigation makes material findings about a living individual or active organisation, we document whether that individual or organisation was given an opportunity to respond to the findings prior to publication. Their response (or refusal to respond) is recorded.

### Accuracy

If we discover an error in a published finding that affects a named individual, we correct it promptly and document the correction as a `Revision`.

---

## What the Platform Is Not For

- Publishing unverified allegations
- Personal vendettas
- Political campaigning
- Targeted harassment
- Publishing information that could expose individuals to physical danger
- Circumventing legal protections (e.g., court-ordered anonymity)

Contributions that serve any of these purposes will not be merged, and the accounts responsible may be banned under the [Code of Conduct](CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md).

## AI Use

See [AI_GUIDELINES.md](AI_GUIDELINES.md) for the specific policy on AI-assisted contributions. The short version: AI may assist in drafting and structuring but may not generate evidence claims, assess confidence levels, or produce source citations without human verification.

## Review

These ethics principles are reviewed by maintainers annually and whenever a novel ethical question arises that is not covered here. Changes are made through the governance process in [GOVERNANCE.md](GOVERNANCE.md) and documented in [DECISIONS.md](DECISIONS.md).
